# Localized metadata from keyword research, 2026-11-20

Nine locales rewritten: it, pt-BR, es-MX, es-ES, en-CA, fr-CA, de-DE, fr-FR, en-GB.
Nothing was pushed to App Store Connect. Not touched: en-US, URLs, release notes,
screenshots, the other 41 locales.

Method: Apple search hints per storefront (autocomplete), iTunes Search API
(top 50 per term, our `trackId` 6811133796 for rank), competitor product pages
for subtitles. Searches ran 2026-11-20. Competitor brand names are not used in
any field.

## What changed everywhere

- Apple's localization table lists en-GB as a secondary locale for Italy,
  Brazil, Mexico, Spain, Germany, Austria, Switzerland, France and Australia.
  So English words ("baby tracker", "timer", "watch", "twins", "formula",
  "shared") already reach those storefronts through en-GB. The local
  keyword fields no longer spend bytes on them (the old fields carried `watch`
  and `timer` in every locale). en-GB keeps those English terms and adds
  `diaper`, the word those storefronts type.
- Canada indexes en-CA plus fr-CA; the US indexes es-MX, pt-BR and fr-FR
  as secondary. Spanish and Portuguese terms in es-MX and pt-BR therefore also
  serve US Hispanic and Brazilian-diaspora searches, and US English is
  covered by en-US.
- Descriptions are rewritten from the current en-US feature list (All button,
  Wet/Dirty rename, reminders, Action button, stain helper, free versus Baby+).
  The old ones predated those features. Subscription, disclaimer and legal
  paragraphs are the live text, unchanged. No medical claims; the disclaimer
  stays.
- Button names follow the app's own catalog (`Shared/Localizable.xcstrings`):
  Tutti, Todos, Alle, Bagnato/Sporco, Molhada/Suja, Mojado/Sucio, Nass/Voll.
  The app ships en, de, es, it and pt-BR; the fr and en-GB/CA listings keep
  the line saying the app is in English.

## Keyword fill (2026-11-21)

Nine locales used under 90 of the 100 keyword characters. Only `keywords.txt` changed; no term was removed. Lengths are characters as `scripts/aso-check-locales.py` counts them (code points, commas included). Nothing was sent to App Store Connect.

Where each field is indexed (Apple's localization table, fetched 2026-11-21): ja is Japan's primary, ko is South Korea's primary and a US secondary, zh-Hans is China's primary and a US and Singapore secondary, zh-Hant is Taiwan's, Hong Kong's and Macau's primary and a US secondary. pl is a secondary in Poland (en-GB is primary there). hi, bn-BD, pa-IN and ur-PK are secondaries in India (ur-PK also in Pakistan), with English (UK) as the primary. So ko, zh-Hans and zh-Hant also reach US Korean and Chinese speakers; the added terms are plain local words, not terms chosen for the US. Download and proceeds numbers per storefront were not in the run prompt, so no field was weighted by them beyond that.

Pump, reminders, widgets, shared log and bottle amount are features the app has (AGENTS.md, en-US description), so those terms are allowed.

### Checks and open questions
`scripts/aso-check-locales.py` on the nine locales: every keyword field 96 to 100, no duplicates, no spaces after commas, no dash characters, no singular/plural pairs. One remaining flag: ur-PK `بچوں کا ڈاکٹر` shares the particle `کا` with the subtitle. It was in the 2026-11-20 sweep as a known false positive and was not touched.
- hi `रोग` ("disease", from `शिशु रोग`) is an existing term that reads as a medical word on its own. It is not a claim, but Jack may want it swapped for `बच्चा`-style words (App Review 1.4.1 caution).
- The Indic and Urdu fields (hi, bn-BD, pa-IN, ur-PK) have no search evidence at all; their value is mostly the India and Pakistan secondary index and is likely small next to the English fields.
- ja `おむつ替え` contains the name's `おむつ`; it is a distinct autocomplete term but Apple may already match it from the name.
