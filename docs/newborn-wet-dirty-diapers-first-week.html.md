# Newborn Wet and Dirty Diapers by Day: First Week Guide

> Day-by-day newborn diaper count for the first week: 1 wet diaper on day 1 rising to 6 or more by day 6, plus what the stools look like and when to call.

## How many wet and dirty diapers should a newborn have each day in the first week?

By [Jack Wallner](https://jackwallner.com/) · Last updated October 18, 2026

For a breastfed newborn, a common guide is at least 1 wet diaper on day 1, 2 on day 2, 3 on day 3, 4 on day 4, 5 on day 5 and 6 or more from day 6, with at least 1 to 2 dirty diapers a day. The American Academy of Pediatrics describes 6 or more wet diapers a day by 5 to 7 days old as one sign of a well-fed baby. These are general ranges, and your pediatrician's advice for your baby comes first.

## How many diapers should a newborn have each day?

For a breastfed newborn, at least one wet diaper per day of life, up to 6 or more from day 6, plus 1 to 2 or more dirty ones. This table follows the NHS Healthier Together guidance for breastfed babies. Each row is the minimum for a 24-hour period, so more is fine.

| Day | Wet diapers | Dirty diapers | What the stool looks like |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 1 or more | 1 or more | Dark green or black and tar-like (meconium) |
| 2 | 2 or more | 1 or more | Dark green or black and tar-like |
| 3 | 3 or more | 2 or more | Changing to brown, green or yellow, and looser |
| 4 | 4 or more | 2 or more | Brown, green or yellow, and looser |
| 5 | 5 or more, heavy | 2 or more | Yellow and runny |
| 6 and after | 6 or more, heavy | 2 or more | Yellow and runny |

The AAP's HealthyChildren.org gives a slightly different framing for the same pattern. It lists 1 or 2 blackish, tarry stools a day on days 1 and 2, at least two stools turning greenish to yellow on days 3 and 4, and yellow, loose stools with small curds at least 3 to 4 times a day by 5 to 7 days old. It also lists 6 or more wet diapers a day, with nearly colorless or pale yellow urine, by 5 to 7 days old. The two sources agree on the direction: counts climb through the first week and settle at 6 or more wet diapers.

## Why do the diaper numbers climb?

They climb as breast milk production increases over the first days. Diapers are a practical way to see feeding going well without measuring anything. The AAP notes that when breast milk production increases, a baby will often have a stool with each feeding for the first month. That is why the dirty diaper count keeps rising after the first few days.

## What diaper details trip parents up?

Rust-colored spots, hard-to-judge disposable diapers and fewer stools later in the first month are the details that worry parents most. The NHS guidance below covers when each one is normal.

### Orange or rust-colored spots

On days 1 and 2 a diaper may contain rust-colored spots called urates, which are concentrated urine. The NHS guidance says they are not a problem at that age. If they continue beyond the first couple of days, it advises telling your midwife (in the US, your pediatrician or lactation consultant) because it can be a sign that a baby is not getting enough milk.

### Disposable diapers are hard to judge

The NHS guidance says it can be hard to tell whether a disposable diaper is wet. Its tip is to pour two to four tablespoons of water into a clean diaper once, so you know what a wet one feels like and how heavy it gets. From day 5 the guidance says to expect heavy wet diapers.

### Later in the first month

After 4 to 6 weeks, when breastfeeding is well established, some breastfed babies go a few days or more without a bowel movement, and the NHS guidance says this can be normal. The wet diaper count should stay at 6 or more a day.

### Formula-fed babies

The ranges above are written for breastfed babies. If your baby is formula-fed or combination-fed, ask your pediatrician what to expect rather than assuming the same numbers.

## When should you call the pediatrician?

Call if the diaper count is below the table for your baby's day, or if you are simply worried. Counts are a guide, not a diagnosis. The NHS Healthier Together guidance says to contact a doctor or nurse the same day if a baby has fewer wet diapers than before, is feeding much less often or for much shorter periods, is not gaining weight or is losing weight, or seems drowsy or irritable.

The same guidance lists signs that need urgent help: a baby who cannot be woken for feeds, is breathing too fast to feed, is pale, mottled and abnormally cold, has blue lips or has a seizure. In the United States, that means calling 911 or going to the nearest emergency department.

The AAP also advises scheduling the first checkup with the pediatrician or family physician no more than 48 hours after hospital discharge. The weight check at that visit helps show whether the baby is getting enough from feedings.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many wet diapers should a newborn have on day 3?

At least 3 wet diapers and 2 dirty diapers in 24 hours, according to the NHS Healthier Together guidance for breastfed babies, with stools changing from dark green or black to brown, green or yellow. Call your pediatrician if your baby has fewer than expected.

### How many wet diapers should a newborn have in 24 hours by the end of the first week?

6 or more wet diapers a day, with nearly colorless or pale yellow urine, by 5 to 7 days old, according to the AAP's HealthyChildren.org. The NHS guidance also gives 6 or more heavy wet diapers a day from day 6.

### How many dirty diapers should a newborn have in the first week?

At least 1 dirty diaper on days 1 and 2 and at least 2 a day from day 3, according to the NHS guidance. The AAP describes yellow, loose stools at least 3 to 4 times a day by 5 to 7 days old.

### Are the numbers different for formula-fed babies?

The ranges on this page come from guidance written for breastfed babies. If your baby is formula-fed or combination-fed, ask your pediatrician what diaper counts to expect.

### What if my newborn has fewer diapers than the table?

Call your pediatrician. The NHS guidance says to contact a doctor or nurse the same day for fewer wet diapers than before, and to seek urgent help if a baby cannot be woken for feeds or has trouble breathing.
