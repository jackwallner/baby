Best Baby Tracker App for iPhone

By Jack Wallner · Last updated October 18, 2026

Best for sleep timing and parenting guidance: Huckleberry, though its SweetSpot nap predictions are in the paid tiers. Best for logging nearly everything, from pumping to vaccines, with core tracking free: Baby Tracker - Newborn Log. Best for twins and for tracking the parent too: Nara. Best for daycare or a nanny on any device, including a web browser: Baby Connect. Best for two iPhone parents who want four buttons and no account: Baby Tracker: Feeds & Diapers, the app I make.

How we compared (October 13, 2026): I read each app's US App Store listing and pulled its rating, rating count, supported devices, purchase types and last update from Apple's store data. I did not install or subscribe to the other apps, so what I say about them comes from their own listings. Baby Tracker: Feeds & Diapers' entry comes from its code and listing. Prices are left out on purpose, because they vary by region and change.

What is each baby tracker app best at?

Huckleberry is best for sleep timing, Baby Tracker - Newborn Log for logging everything, Nara for twins and parent self-care, Baby Connect for daycare and nannies, and Baby Tracker: Feeds & Diapers for fast logging with no account.

Huckleberry: Baby Tracker

The highest-rated app in this group. The free tier covers one-touch logging for sleep, diapers, feeds, pumping, growth, milestones and medicine, a breastfeeding timer for both sides, several children and sync between caregivers. Plus adds SweetSpot, which suggests nap and sleep times from 2 months, plus sleep schedules, reports and AI logging; Premium adds an AI chat called Berry and sleep plans. The trade-off: the feature it is best known for is paid, and much of the paid tier leans on AI guidance.

Baby Tracker - Newborn Log

The most-rated baby tracker on the US store. It logs feeds (including pumping and solids), sleep, wet, dirty and mixed diapers, growth against WHO standards, medicines, vaccinations, temperature and milestones with photos, and exports PDF reports. It has an Apple Watch app, widgets, Live Activities, Siri and sync between devices. The trade-off: the free version shows ads, and its What's Next timing cues need a Plus subscription after a short preview.

Nara Baby & Pregnancy Tracker

Nara has a calm design and was built by a mom to track her own newborn. It logs feeds, pumping, solids, diapers, sleep, growth and medical records, compares twins, and tracks the parent's pregnancy and postpartum health. Partners and caregivers join through a Nara account, and it works on Apple Watch. The trade-off: no iPad support is listed, and the listing does not say which features are paid.

Baby Connect: Newborn Tracker

Baby Connect runs on every platform, including web browsers, which makes it a fit for daycare, nannies and families with mixed phones. It logs feeds, nursing, naps, diapers, pumping, milestones and health data, with percentile comparisons and push updates. The trade-off: after the 7-day trial a subscription is required to keep saving entries, with read-only access otherwise.

Baby Tracker: Feeds & Diapers (my app)

Four buttons that never move: Feed (left, right or bottle), Pee, Poop and Sleep. One tap logs it, a long press sets the time, side, amount or stool colour. Widgets log with one tap, and there is an Apple Watch app with a time-since-last-feed complication, a Live Activity and Siri. Logging together works by scanning a code, with no account: records stay on your devices and in your own iCloud. A first-weeks diaper tally and more than one baby are free; Baby+ adds the pediatrician PDF, trends and CSV export. Its limits: every caregiver needs an iPhone (no Android or web), it does not log pumping, solids, medicine or milestones, it has no sleep predictions, and it has no ratings yet.

Which should you pick?

Pick by the problem you have most at 3am: sleep timing, logging everything, twins, outside caregivers, or the fewest taps.

  • You want help with naps and wake windows: Huckleberry.
  • You want to log everything, from pumping to temperature, and do not mind ads: Baby Tracker - Newborn Log.
  • You have twins, or want to track your own recovery alongside the baby: Nara.
  • A daycare, a nanny or an Android phone is part of the team: Baby Connect.
  • Both parents have iPhones and you want the fewest taps at 3am, with no sign-up: Baby Tracker: Feeds & Diapers.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best free baby tracker app?

All five are free to download. Huckleberry and Baby Tracker - Newborn Log keep core tracking free, with ads in Newborn Log's free version. Baby Tracker: Feeds & Diapers keeps logging, sharing, widgets and the Apple Watch app free. Baby Connect needs a subscription to keep saving entries after its 7-day trial.

Which baby tracker app can both parents use?

All five sync between caregivers, per their listings. Nara invites partners to a Nara account. Baby Tracker: Feeds & Diapers shares through iCloud with a scanned code and no account, which means every caregiver needs an iPhone.

Which baby tracker apps work on Apple Watch?

All five support Apple Watch. Baby Tracker: Feeds & Diapers adds a complication that shows how long it has been since the last feed.

Can a baby tracker app tell me if something is wrong?

No. These apps are logs. Bring the numbers to your pediatrician and call them with any concern about feeding, diapers or sleep.

What is the best app for tracking baby feeding?

It depends on how you feed. Huckleberry's free tier includes a breastfeeding timer for both sides plus feeds and pumping, and Baby Tracker - Newborn Log logs feeds including pumping and solids. Baby Tracker: Feeds & Diapers logs a feed (left, right or bottle) in one tap, shares it with your partner, and shows the time since the last feed on Apple Watch, but it does not log pumping or solids.

Which baby tracker is better, Baby Tracker or Huckleberry?

If you mean Baby Tracker - Newborn Log, the most-rated tracker on the US store, it is the better pick for logging nearly everything (pumping, solids, vaccines, temperature, milestones), with core tracking free and ads in the free version. Huckleberry is the better pick if naps and sleep timing matter most, though its SweetSpot nap predictions are in the paid tiers. My app, Baby Tracker: Feeds & Diapers, is a different and simpler app: four buttons for feeds, diapers and sleep, shared through iCloud with no account.
